# 05 - Audit of every calculation and assumption (2026-10-08)

Requested after the catchment-area error was found: re-verify the methodology, look for other mistakes, and check
that our assumptions match how NAACC actually defines things. Scripts: `scripts/plunkett_audit_checks.py`,
`scripts/plunkett_component_recheck.py`, `scripts/plunkett_dev_rerun_fixed_cv.py`, `scripts/plunkett_state_transfer.py`,
`scripts/download_naacc_public_layer.py`, `scripts/validate_osm_pbf.py`. Raw numbers: `results/plunkett/`.

## B. Independent checks that passed (or refined a claim)

**B1. Which catchment area is right? (label-free)** Field-measured bankfull width (public NAACC layer; independent of
both areas and of the score): Spearman with `d8accum` 0.667 vs 0.343 with the old NLDI area (n = 1,840; high-confidence
widths only: 0.669 vs 0.398; width 5-15 ft: 0.315 vs 0.108). On NBI-matched (larger-stream) crossings the two are close
(structure length: 0.631 vs 0.604). So the old area was acceptable on big streams and poor on small ones. The
`d8accum` 30 m cell size is an **assumption** (the DSL document states 30 m for the output grid; `d8accum`'s own
resolution is not documented). Tree models are invariant to the unit, and no reported number depends on it.

**B2. Is Plunkett's target the same as NAACC's published score?** Public layer vs Plunkett by survey ID: 27,459
surveys in common; **99.6% identical (|diff| < 0.001), r = 0.9995**. 49 differ by > 0.05 (13 by > 0.2). Causes were not
investigated: a few have different survey dates in the two files, 8 are -1 ("not scored") in the older public layer, and
others differ with identical dates (possibly later edits or re-scoring).

**B3. Component predictability, corrected features** (NY development crossings, HUC8 CV, 3 splits; components from the
reconstruction): openness 0.49, height 0.49, outlet drop 0.20, constriction 0.15, substrate coverage 0.09 - learnable;
physical barriers -0.05, water depth 0.03, water velocity 0.02, scour pool -0.01, substrate match -0.02 - not
predictable. (Inlet grade, armoring and internal structures had too few distinct values / rows to test.)
**Important refinement:** NAACC's *published* score is predictable at R2 0.353 on this subset, but my reconstruction of it
only at 0.224 (composite 0.167). The reconstruction lacks NAACC's type rules (see B7), which GIS captures. So the
"practical ceiling near 0.27-0.31" argument in the WRITEUP **understated what GIS can capture** and should be read as a
hypothesis about the *formula components*, not a bound on the published score. The best honest number remains the locked
test (0.286 -> 0.320).

**B4. NBI match precision** (NY, stream-name agreement of the nearest NBI structure vs a shuffled baseline of 1.5%):
81% within 10 m, 77% at 10-30 m, 66% at 30-60 m, 54% at 60-100 m, 47% at 100-200 m, 41% at 200-500 m. NBI coordinates
are imprecise: some 60 m matches are wrong, and some true matches lie farther away. R2 is flat across radii 30-150 m, so
the effect on results is small, but the match is noisy. A name-aware match is not possible because Plunkett's file has no
stream names.

**B5. Structure newer than the survey.** NBI is a 2025 snapshot. 398 of 9,248 matched crossings (4.3%) list a build year
after the survey year (189 a reconstruction year) - their label describes a structure that has since been replaced.
Small label noise; not removed.

**B6. Geography.** HUC8 polygons are generalised (~100 m): 150 of 56,525 crossings (0.27%) lie within 150 m of a
boundary, 1 point matched two polygons, none matched zero. Negligible.

**B7. How NAACC defines the target.** Scores are partly *rules*, not measurements: all 4,257 "Bridge Adequate" crossings
score exactly 1.0 and all 603 "Buried Stream" exactly 0.1. NAACC's manual: "Bridge Adequate: Coordinators have the
option of using this classification for large bridges for which it is obvious that they present no barrier."
It is an optional coordinator judgement, and **its use varies by state**: share of surveyed bridges labelled "Bridge
Adequate" is NH 0%, RI 0%, VT 1%, NJ 9%, CT 9%, MA 11%, VA 22%, NY 25%, PA 47%, WV 58%, ME 63%, MD 83%. This is a concrete,
documented example of the state-level labelling differences Ethan described, and it cannot be seen from GIS data.
Also: `bridge` in the analysis scripts = Bridge + Bridge Adequate; 61 "Removed Crossing" rows remain (mean 0.89).

**B8. Locked-test integrity.** Same 7 HUC6 basins as recorded before modelling; disjoint crossing and survey IDs; no locked
crossing within 50 m of a development crossing; no identical predictor vectors on both sides. Locked R2 by distance to
the nearest development crossing: 0.30 / 0.33 / 0.34 / 0.28 / 0.30 (nearest to farthest fifth) - **no distance trend, so
no sign of spatial leakage inflating the score.** sklearn's `r2_score` reproduces the reported 0.3202.

**B9. What Plunkett's filtering removed.** Public layer, valid scores, one record per crossing: 69% are in Plunkett's
file; reasons for the rest were not determined (the layer is an older snapshot, and Plunkett kept only crossings on the DSL
networks). Dropped vs kept mean scores are
similar (culverts 0.518 vs 0.528, bridges 0.853 vs 0.883, multiple culverts 0.573 vs 0.628), so the filtering does not
obviously bias the score distribution.

**B10. OSM PBF parser** reproduces Overpass on Rhode Island: 100% per-crossing agreement for bridges, 99.8% for culverts.

## D. Still unverified / assumptions to keep in mind

1. **Surveyed is not the population.** All R2 values describe surveyed crossings. Whether the unsurveyed crossings
   the model would be applied to resemble them (survey effort is chosen by programs and volunteers) cannot be tested
   with these data. This is the largest untested assumption for deployment.
2. `d8accum` resolution (30 m assumed), `tsurveyed = 2` (1,546 rows; meaning undocumented) and how Plunkett's pipeline
   chose one survey per crossing are not documented in the file; no result depends on them.
3. NBI/OSM are present-day snapshots; the surveys span 2003-2026.
4. OSM completeness varies by state (recall of surveyed bridges 45-85%); NBI by structure size (>= 6.1 m only).
5. Tuned-model development scores carry a small selection bias (tuned on the same development set); the locked-test
   numbers are the clean ones. The tuned-vs-default gain is +0.003-0.004 either way.
6. The 40 locked watersheds limit statistical power: the OSM step (+0.005 locked, +0.010 development) cannot be
   called established.
7. NJ is poorly predicted in every setup (R2 about 0.1); cause not identified.
8. Only Northeast / Mid-Atlantic states are covered. Wider deployment is untested.
9. A public NAACC layer exists without login (53,173 records, same 13 states + DC, snapshot ending ~2020) but adds
   no training data beyond Plunkett's file; NAACC data for other regions was not found in a public, downloadable form.
